What Size Tank Do Gouramis Need?

"Gourami" covers fish from the 1.5 inch sparkling gourami to the giant gourami, which outgrows almost any home aquarium. The small species most people buy, honey and dwarf gouramis, are commonly kept in tanks from about 10 US gallons, with 20 gallons more forgiving. Pearl and three spot gouramis need around 30 gallons or more.

Sizes and minimums vary between sources and individual fish; these are cautious starting points for one fish or a pair.

Temperament

  • Male gouramis are often territorial toward each other. Many keepers keep only one male dwarf gourami per tank unless it is large and well planted.
  • Honey, sparkling and pearl gouramis are generally among the more peaceful species.
  • Three spot gouramis can become pushy as they grow, and larger gouramis may eat very small fish or shrimp.
  • Gouramis and bettas are both labyrinth fish and are commonly kept apart because of aggression.
  • Avoid fin-nipping tank mates such as tiger barbs; gouramis' long feeler fins are an easy target.

Care notes

  • Gouramis breathe air from the surface with a labyrinth organ, so leave surface access and keep a lid on to hold warm, humid air above the water.
  • Most common species are kept around 75-80 F (24-27 C); size a heater with the heater guide and check ranges in the temperature guide.
  • They prefer gentle flow and floating plants; males of several species build bubble nests.
  • Dwarf gouramis are widely reported to be prone to a viral disease (dwarf gourami iridovirus), so buy healthy-looking fish and use a quarantine tank.
