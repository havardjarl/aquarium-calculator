How Many Fish Can Go in a 10 Gallon Tank?

A 10 gallon tank (typically 20 x 10 x 12 in) holds closer to 8-9 gallons of water once substrate and decor are in. That is enough for one small, well-planned community, not a mix of everything at the store. Pick one main group of small fish, maybe a few shrimp, and stop there.

The limits behind the checker are rules of thumb for a filtered, cycled, freshwater 10 gallon with weekly water changes. They are deliberately cautious and not a guarantee; individual fish, filtration and maintenance all matter.

ShrimpA cherry shrimp colony, starting with ~10 and letting it grow

Mixing groups is possible, but reduce each group so the total stays within one tank's worth.

Fish that do not belong in a 10 gallon

  • Goldfish: too much waste and they grow too large.
  • Angelfish, oscars, discus and most cichlids.
  • Common plecos, which can grow over a foot long.
  • Standard corydoras and mollies, usually kept in 20 gallons or more.
  • Most gouramis other than small species (see the gourami tank size guide).

Making a 10 gallon work

  • Finish a fishless cycle first, then add fish a few at a time.
  • Small tanks swing quickly, so keep up with weekly water changes and avoid overfeeding (see how much to feed fish).
  • Use a heater for tropical fish (see the heater size guide) and a gentle filter.
