How Many Fish Can Go in a 20 Gallon Tank?

A 20 gallon tank holds roughly 17-18 gallons of water once substrate and decor are in. That is enough for one centerpiece fish or pair, one school of small fish, and a small bottom group, all kept small. It is the size many keepers recommend as a first tank because water quality swings more slowly than in a 5 or 10 gallon.

20 long vs 20 high

TankTypical outside sizeBest for
20 gallon long30 x 12 x 12 inActive schooling fish, corydoras and other bottom dwellers that need floor space
20 gallon high24 x 12 x 16 inTaller fish and planted layouts, but less swimming length and floor space

Both hold about the same water, but the long version has about 25% more floor space, which matters more for stocking than the extra height. Check your exact dimensions with the aquarium calculator.

Conservative 20 gallon stocking plans

PlanRoughly
Nano community~10-12 neon or ember tetras, ~6 pygmy or panda corydoras, a few cherry shrimp or a snail
Gourami centerpiece1 dwarf or honey gourami, ~8-10 small tetras or rasboras, ~6 small corydoras
Livebearers~6-8 platies or guppies (ideally single-sex) and ~6 small corydoras
Danio tank (20 long)~8-10 zebra danios and ~6 corydoras
Betta community1 male betta, ~6-8 calm small fish such as ember tetras or chili rasboras, and a few corydoras
Pea puffers~5-6 in a heavily planted, species-only tank

Pick one plan rather than combining them. These are cautious rules of thumb for a filtered, cycled freshwater tank with weekly water changes, not a guarantee.

Fish that do not belong in a 20 gallon

  • Goldfish (especially fancy groups or common goldfish) produce a lot of waste and outgrow it.
  • Angelfish are usually kept in taller tanks of 30 gallons or more, and oscars, discus and most African cichlids need much more.
  • Common plecos, which can grow over a foot long. A small species such as a bristlenose is the usual alternative.
  • Larger gouramis such as three spot and kissing gouramis (see the gourami tank size guide).

Making a 20 gallon work

  • Cycle the tank first (see the cycling guide), then add fish a few at a time over several weeks.
  • Size the filter for roughly 4-6 turnovers per hour, about 80-120 gallons per hour (see the filter flow guide), and a heater around 75-100 W for most rooms (see the heater guide).
  • Keep up with regular water changes and avoid overfeeding.
  • A full 20 gallon weighs roughly 225 lb, so use a proper stand (see the tank weight chart).
