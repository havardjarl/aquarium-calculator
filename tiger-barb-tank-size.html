What Size Tank Do Tiger Barbs Need?

Tiger barbs grow to about 2.5-3 inches and are very active. A common recommendation is a group of at least 6 in a tank of at least 20-30 US gallons, with a 30 gallon or a 20 gallon long giving them the swimming length they use.

Group size by tank (rule of thumb)

TankTiger barbs, if they are the main fish
20 gal long~6
29-30 gal~6-8
40 gal breeder~8-12
55 gal~10-15

These are cautious starting points; reduce them when you keep other fish in the same tank.

Why group size matters

Tiger barbs are well known for nipping fins. In a group of 6 or more they mostly chase each other, while a pair or trio often turns on other fish. A bigger group and plenty of plants and decor usually spread that energy out.

Tank mates

  • Often paired with fast, robust fish such as zebra danios, other barbs, larger corydoras or a bristlenose pleco.
  • Commonly avoided with slow or long-finned fish: bettas, angelfish, fancy guppies and gouramis.

Care notes

  • Tiger barbs are commonly kept around 74-80 F (23-27 C); see the temperature guide and the heater guide.
  • They are hardy but still need a fully cycled tank (see the cycling guide).
  • They like moderate flow; size the filter with the filter flow guide.
