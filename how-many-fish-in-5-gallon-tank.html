How Many Fish Can Go in a 5 Gallon Tank?

A 5 gallon tank holds only about 4 gallons of water after substrate and decor. That is enough for one small fish with a big personality, or a shrimp colony, not a community. Small tanks are harder to keep stable, because ammonia and temperature change quickly in a small volume of water.

Good options for 5 gallons

OptionRoughly
Single betta1 male or female betta, optionally a nerite snail
Shrimp colonyCherry or other neocaridina shrimp, starting with ~10 (see the shrimp tank guide)
Pea puffer1 pea puffer in a well-planted tank (see the pea puffer guide)
Snails onlyA few nerite or mystery snails

Many keepers advise against schooling fish such as neon tetras, ember tetras or rasboras in 5 gallons, because they need a group of at least 6 and room to swim. A 10 gallon is the usual starting point for them.

Fish to avoid in a 5 gallon

  • Goldfish of any kind, including small fancy goldfish.
  • Guppies, platies and mollies, which breed quickly and soon overfill a small tank (see the guppy guide).
  • Zebra danios and other fast swimmers (see the zebra danio guide).
  • Corydoras, which need a group and floor space (see the corydoras guide).

Keeping a 5 gallon stable

  • Cycle it before adding livestock (see the cycling guide) and test ammonia and nitrite often (see the ammonia calculator).
  • Change about 20-30% of the water weekly (see how often to change water) and feed sparingly (see how much to feed fish).
  • Use a small adjustable heater, around 25 W for most rooms, for a betta or other tropical fish (see the heater size guide).
  • Choose a gentle filter; bettas and shrimp do best with low flow.
